ℹ️ About Pickleball

Pickleball is a paddle sport that blends elements of tennis, badminton, and table tennis. It's played on a small court with a perforated plastic ball and solid paddles, either as singles (1 vs 1) or doubles (2 vs 2 — what this scoreboard is built for).

Court Layout

Key Rules

  • Serve: Underhand, diagonally cross-court, from behind the baseline. The ball must clear the net and land in the opponent's diagonal service court, beyond the non-volley zone.
  • Two-bounce rule: After the serve, the receiving side must let the ball bounce once before returning it, and the serving side must also let that return bounce once before volleying — each side gets one groundstroke before volleys are allowed.
  • Non-volley zone ("the kitchen"): The 7 ft zone on each side of the net. Players cannot volley (hit the ball out of the air) while standing inside it, or if their momentum carries them in immediately after.
  • Scoring (traditional side-out, doubles): Only the serving team can score. Each doubles team gets two servers per turn, except the very first serve of each game (only one server before the first side-out). Games are usually to 11, win by 2.
  • Side-out: When the serving team loses a rally on their second server (or their only server at the game's first turn), the serve passes to the other team.
  • Faults: Hitting the ball out of bounds or into the net, volleying from the kitchen, or serving illegally all end the rally as a fault.

Glossary

KitchenNickname for the non-volley zone.
DinkA soft, arcing shot hit from near the kitchen line into the opponent's kitchen.
Side-outLoss of serve from one team/side to the other.
RallyThe continuous exchange of shots until a fault ends the point.
ErneAn advanced shot where a player jumps around the kitchen (outside the court) to volley near the net.
